# Q1 — Use Case Design: AI-Assisted Legal Intelligence at a Mid-Size Spanish Law Firm

## Context

**Industry**: Legal services — corporate and commercial law.
**Company**: Garriga & Associats, SLP — 80 employees (32 lawyers, 18 paralegals, 6 IT/admin,
24 support) in Barcelona, operating under Spanish Bar Association rules and full GDPR
jurisdiction as an EU data processor.

The firm handles ~200 client matters per month, 60–80 requiring intensive document work:
NDA reviews, commercial contract drafting, M&A due diligence, and internal case law research
against a proprietary corpus of 12,000+ annotated Spanish and EU court decisions accumulated
over 22 years of practice.

**The problem today**: Contract review is bottlenecked at the associate level. A senior
associate billing at €180/hour spends 3.5 hours on a standard NDA — identifying non-standard
clauses, cross-referencing internal precedents, flagging GDPR-sensitive terms, and drafting
a partner summary. At 50 NDAs/month that is 175 associate-hours (~€31,500/month) that cannot
be fully billed back because clients resist paying commodity-review rates. Outsourcing to a
Big Four legal-tech vendor is blocked: client documents under confidentiality agreements
cannot be processed on a shared SaaS platform without explicit consent that is structurally
impossible to obtain at intake.

**Quantified business case.** Reducing Elena's NDA review from 3.5 h to 1.5 h saves 2 h
per document. At 50 NDAs/month that is **100 associate-hours recovered** — worth **€18,000/month**
in redeployable senior capacity. The infrastructure cost of a `t3.xlarge` in `eu-west-1`
(4 vCPU / 16 GB RAM / 60 GB gp3 EBS) is **~$200/month all-in** (~€185), yielding a
**97:1 return on infrastructure spend**. Even at half the saving (1 h/NDA), the case is
€9,000 recovered against €185 spent. This is a structural reallocation of the firm's most
expensive resource, not a marginal efficiency gain.

The firm evaluated ChatGPT Enterprise and Microsoft Copilot for Legal and rejected both
within a week of piloting for reasons detailed in §4.

## Personas

**Persona 1 — Elena Vidal, Senior Associate (6 years PQE)**
Reviews 8–12 NDAs/month alongside litigation work. Workflow today: receive PDF by email →
open in Adobe → manually cross-reference the firm's internal clause library (a Notion
database) → draft a Word memo → route to partner. Pain points: clause library is
unsearchable beyond keywords; she must remember or stumble upon precedents; memo drafting
is repetitive but non-delegatable because only she can interpret the legal risk.

**Persona 2 — Marc Soler, Paralegal (3 years experience)**
First-pass document intake: receives client files, classifies them, uploads to the DMS,
extracts basic metadata (parties, effective date, governing law). Pain points: legacy
SharePoint DMS has no AI-assist; Marc cannot flag risky clauses, so everything escalates
to Elena regardless of complexity. He estimates 30% of files are low-risk and could be
cleared without senior review given a reliable screening tool.

**Persona 3 — Jordi Mas, IT & Compliance Officer**
Responsible for GDPR Article 32 technical measures and the firm's ISO 27001 roadmap. His
constraint is absolute: no client data may leave the firm's AWS eu-west-1 VPC. Every SaaS
tool requires a DPA and a Transfer Impact Assessment. Cloud AI services are rejected as
policy, consistent with AEPD guidance: when a professional uses an external AI tool at
work, the organisation remains the data controller and transmitting client data to that
tool may constitute unlawful processing under GDPR Art. 6 [AEPD Decálogo, 2023].

## Journey

All containers run on a single EC2 `t3.xlarge` in `eu-west-1`.

**Step 1 — Authentication (Casdoor → PostgreSQL)**
Marc opens `https://<firm-domain>` and authenticates through **Casdoor** (port 47002) via
SAML federation to the firm's Active Directory. Casdoor writes the session token to
**PostgreSQL** (port 47003, `casdoor` DB). LobeChat validates the JWT on every request.
No credentials leave the EC2 instance.

**Step 2 — Document Upload (LobeChat → MinIO)**
Marc uploads the NDA PDF via the LobeChat interface. LobeChat streams it to **MinIO**
(port 47005), stored as `matters/2025-0412/nda-counterparty.pdf` in the `legal-docs`
bucket. PostgreSQL records the reference in LobeChat's `files` table. File is at rest
inside the VPC; zero egress.

**Step 3 — Ingestion (MCPHub → haystack-mcp → Hayhooks → Qdrant)**
Marc types: *"Ingest this NDA into the review pipeline."* LobeChat routes to **vLLM**
(port 47007, **Gemma 4 E4B**, native function calling). Gemma 4 emits a tool call to
**MCPHub** (port 47008) targeting `haystack-mcp`, which dispatches to **Hayhooks-MCP**
(port 47013). The `nda_ingestion` Haystack pipeline fetches the PDF from MinIO, splits
it into 512-token chunks (64-token overlap), generates embeddings, and upserts 47 chunks
with metadata (`matter_id`, `document_type: NDA`) into the **Qdrant** `legal-rag`
collection (port 47010). Hayhooks returns `{ingested: 47, matter_id: "2025-0412"}`;
Gemma 4 confirms to Marc in plain language.

**Step 4 — Clause Review (vLLM → MCPHub → qdrant-mcp → Qdrant)**
Elena asks: *"Identify non-standard limitation-of-liability clauses and compare to our
standard position."* Gemma 4 issues two sequential `qdrant-mcp` calls via MCPHub: one
scoped to `matter_id: 2025-0412`, one to `document_type: standard-clause-library`. qdrant-mcp
executes cosine-similarity search and returns top-5 chunks per query. Gemma 4 receives
~3,000 tokens of context, identifies a unilateral cap at 3× annual fees versus the firm's
standard 1× direct damages, and drafts a risk memo with clause citations and PDF page
numbers from Qdrant metadata. Round-trip: ~8–12 seconds.

**Step 5 — Case Law Search (MCPHub → qdrant-mcp, `case-law` collection)**
Elena asks for supporting Spanish decisions. Gemma 4 queries the `case-law` Qdrant
collection (12,000 embedded decisions), retrieves 4 Audiencia Provincial rulings from
2019–2023, and synthesises a cited argument — replacing ~45 minutes of manual CENDOJ
search.

**Step 6 — Notion Sync (MCPHub → notion-mcp)**
Gemma 4 calls `notion-mcp` via MCPHub to write the review outcome, risk rating (Medium),
and MinIO memo link into the firm's matter-management database. No copy-paste required.

**Step 7 — Audit Trail (PostgreSQL)**
Every tool call, model response, and file reference is persisted in LobeChat's PostgreSQL
`messages` table with user ID, timestamp, and model version — a GDPR Article 30 record
of processing fulfilled automatically.

## Why This Stack vs. ChatGPT Enterprise / Microsoft Copilot

**Reason 1 — Absolute Data Sovereignty, No DPA Required**
ChatGPT Enterprise processes data on OpenAI's US infrastructure. GDPR Chapter V requires
a Transfer Impact Assessment for US transfers. In March 2023 the Italian Garante banned
ChatGPT outright for absence of legal basis for personal data processing [Garante,
Provvedimento 30 marzo 2023]. With this stack every byte — the NDA, embeddings, model
weights, session tokens — stays inside the firm's eu-west-1 VPC. No DPA to negotiate,
no TIA to maintain, no cross-border transfer exposure.

**Reason 2 — Custom RAG on 22 Years of Proprietary Case Law**
ChatGPT and Copilot have no access to the firm's corpus of 12,000 annotated decisions and
8,000 clause templates — uploading them to a shared SaaS would be a confidentiality breach.
Qdrant on EC2 with Hayhooks ingestion pipelines makes this corpus a first-class retrieval
asset, updated continuously. The `qdrant-mcp` integration surfaces it in any LobeChat
conversation without a separate tool switch. The corpus grows in value over time; that
value cannot migrate to a SaaS alternative.

**Reason 3 — MCP Tool Integrations That SaaS Cannot Replicate**
MCPHub exposes capabilities unavailable in any SaaS AI product: `ssh-exec` for compliance
scripts on the EC2 host; `notion-mcp` for bidirectional matter-database sync without
leaving chat; `filesystem` for reading documents from a mounted NFS share; `aws-resources`
for querying S3 access logs during data-subject access requests. Microsoft Copilot requires
Microsoft 365 connectors and cannot be extended with arbitrary stdio MCP servers inside
the firm's VPC. LobeChat + MCPHub is a unified AI interface to the firm's entire technical
estate, not a chat layer on top of a productivity suite.

**Non-Obvious Trade-Off**
This architecture shifts operational burden onto Jordi: model updates, Qdrant snapshots,
EC2 patching, Casdoor identity management. ChatGPT Enterprise eliminates that burden
entirely. The bet holds for a firm of 80 with a dedicated IT officer; it should be
revisited above ~300 employees, at which point AWS Bedrock with private model deployment
becomes cost-competitive with the ops overhead of self-hosting vLLM.
