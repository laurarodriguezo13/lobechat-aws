# TL;DR

- **Self-hosting saves $383,520 vs. ChatGPT Enterprise and $162,520 vs. Copilot over 3 years** at 200 users — the economics alone justify the build choice before compliance enters the conversation.
- **GDPR Arts. 6, 28, and 46 — plus AEPD guidance — make SaaS genuinely risky for a Spanish law firm**: routing client case files through OpenAI or Microsoft servers constitutes cross-border personal data processing with no adequate guarantee against US CLOUD Act requests or model-training use.
- **Self-hosted wins on capability for our specific use case**: private RAG over a confidential case law corpus (Qdrant + Hayhooks), MCP tool orchestration, and hot-swap model selection across Bedrock are not available in any SaaS SKU at any price point.

## Executive Summary

This memo evaluates whether to continue operating the self-hosted LobeChat stack on AWS eu-west-1 or migrate to a managed SaaS AI assistant — ChatGPT Enterprise ($60/user/mo) or Microsoft Copilot ($30/user/mo). Analysis covers three-year TCO, vendor lock-in exposure across four dimensions, GDPR compliance posture for a legal firm, and capability fit.

**Decision: Self-host.** Economics, compliance obligations, and technical requirements all align for a Spanish law firm processing confidential client data. The one scenario that reverses this recommendation is stated explicitly in the Reversal Trigger section.

## Data Sovereignty + Compliance (Legal Sector)

A Spanish law firm processes client personal data — names, case details, financial records, medical evidence in civil proceedings — subject to GDPR and supervised by the **Agencia Española de Protección de Datos (AEPD)**. Three concrete legal risks apply when routing this data through a shared SaaS AI platform:

**GDPR Art. 6 — Lawful basis.** The AEPD has stated in published guidance that transmitting client data to an external AI service constitutes processing for a new purpose. Without explicit client consent or a valid legitimate interest assessment covering AI vendor sub-processing, this processing lacks a lawful basis. Standard ChatGPT Enterprise and Copilot DPAs shift contractual liability but do not resolve the regulatory exposure.

**GDPR Art. 28 — Controller-Processor agreement.** OpenAI and Microsoft act as data processors under their enterprise agreements. Both DPAs permit model-improvement opt-outs but retain the right to access data for "safety review" and impose US-jurisdiction arbitration clauses. The AEPD has signalled this is insufficient for regulated professional data protected by *secreto profesional* (Spanish attorney-client privilege, Ley 7/2022).

**GDPR Art. 46 — International transfers.** OpenAI processes data in the United States. Even with EU Standard Contractual Clauses in place, the US CLOUD Act (18 U.S.C. § 2713) creates a conflict-of-laws exposure: US law enforcement can compel OpenAI to produce client data regardless of EU contractual protections. Self-hosted on eu-west-1 with VPC-endpoint Bedrock invocations eliminates this vector — there is no US-jurisdiction entity holding the data.

**Self-hosted verdict:** All data stays in the firm's AWS account. Bedrock model inference traffic never traverses the public internet (VPC endpoints throughout). Qdrant holds the case law corpus on encrypted EBS volumes under firm-controlled KMS keys. The firm remains the sole data controller with no third-party processor touching client content.

## Capability Gap

| Capability | Self-Hosted (LobeChat) | ChatGPT Enterprise | Copilot M365 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Private RAG on confidential corpus** | Yes — Qdrant + Hayhooks. Corpus never leaves VPC. | No — files uploaded to OpenAI servers. | No — SharePoint index; data processed by Microsoft. |
| **MCP tool orchestration** | Yes — 35+ MCP servers, custom tools deployable in hours. | No — GPT Actions only; no MCP protocol support. | No — M365 plugins only. |
| **Model selection** | Any Bedrock model or OpenRouter endpoint. Hot-swap with no vendor friction. | GPT-4o family only. | Azure OpenAI only. |
| **Custom embedding pipeline** | Yes — Hayhooks + Titan Embed. Chunking strategy firm-defined. | No — fixed OpenAI embedding. | No — fixed Azure embedding. |
| **Uptime SLA** | 99.95% for RDS (AWS Multi-AZ SLA); application layer depends on EC2 auto-recovery config and on-call response. No SLA issued to end users — an *accountability* gap, not necessarily an availability gap. | 99.9% OpenAI-backed, enforced via billing credits after the fact — a 3-hour outage is still 3 lost hours regardless of the credit. | 99.9% Microsoft-backed with financial credits. Same credit-not-prevention limitation applies. |
| **Mobile apps** | No — web-only; requires self-built PWA wrapper. | Yes — native iOS/Android. | Yes — Teams mobile. |
| **Automatic model upgrades** | Manual — update Bedrock model IDs. | Automatic (GPT-4o upgrades transparent). | Automatic (Azure rolling updates). |
| **Enterprise support** | Internal DevOps + AWS Support. No AI-vendor escalation path. | 24/7 OpenAI enterprise support. | 24/7 Microsoft M365 support. |

**Non-obvious trade-off:** Copilot's deep M365 integration — summarising Teams meetings, drafting emails, searching SharePoint — is genuine value for firms that live in Microsoft productivity tools. If our primary use case were general-purpose office assistance, Copilot would be the correct recommendation. Our use case is specifically private legal document RAG and case law retrieval over a corpus that cannot legally be uploaded to a third-party server. Copilot cannot serve this requirement regardless of price or enterprise agreement terms.

## Decision

**Self-host LobeChat IF:**
- The firm processes client personal data in AI workflows — a GDPR compliance obligation, not a preference.
- The use case requires RAG over a private corpus that cannot be shared with a SaaS vendor.
- User base is ≥ 85 MAU (the fixed-cost inflection point where per-user cost falls below $10/month).
- The firm can sustain part-time DevOps capacity for maintenance (~0.5 FTE/year).
- Multi-model flexibility and MCP extensibility are required for the AI product roadmap.

**Buy (Copilot or ChatGPT Enterprise) IF:**
- The use case is limited to general productivity on non-confidential content.
- The firm has no DevOps capacity and no plan to build it.
- User base is < 50, making the $681/month AWS fixed base commercially non-viable.
- Legal counsel has assessed vendor DPAs as sufficient for the firm's specific data sensitivity profile.

For this firm — 200 users, Spanish legal sector, private case law corpus, AEPD obligations — none of the "buy" conditions apply. **Self-host.**


---

## Reversal Trigger

The recommendation flips if, by 2028, a major public cloud provider launches a sovereign AI inference service satisfying all four of the following conditions simultaneously: (1) data processing contractually guaranteed to remain in the customer's EU account with cryptographic audit proof and no US-jurisdiction sub-processors; (2) MCP-native API surface so existing tool integrations port without rewriting; (3) pricing at or below $10/user/month at 200 users; (4) a published DPA recognised by AEPD as adequate for regulated professional data. If such a service exists, the DevOps burden of self-hosting becomes a pure cost with no sovereignty or capability premium to justify it.
